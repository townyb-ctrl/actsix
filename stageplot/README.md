# Stageplot

**Stage plots, patching and power for church production teams, in minutes instead of an evening.**

Every week a volunteer tech or worship leader works out who is on stage, where their gear goes, which stage-box input each mic lands on, what plugs into which circuit, and how much cable to pull, usually across a whiteboard, a spreadsheet and memory. Stageplot does it in one place:

1. **Add the team.** Import who's scheduled from Planning Center, paste a list, or type names.
2. **Build the stage.** One click places everyone's gear by position on your real stage shape: kit, guitars and basses on stands, amps, pedals, keys, mics, wedges or in-ears, with jack leads run from each instrument through its pedals to its amp or DI.
3. **It's patched and powered.** Inputs land on the nearest stage box in band order, outputs feed mains, subs and IEMs, and every powered item is put on the least-loaded circuit, with load checks.
4. **Print the paperwork.** Stage plot, input list, outputs, monitor mixes, cable pull list, power sheet and team, as one PDF or a single file to share.

Then drag anything, run cables by hand, hide layers, and next week start from a copy.

**For** church sound, production and worship teams, from a volunteer's first Sunday to a production manager running several services. Simple view keeps everyday controls up front; Advanced shows every setting.

**How it ships.** A single HTML file: no install, no account, no server. Weeks are saved in the browser; backup and restore moves them between computers. Planning Center access is optional and read-only.

**What's verified** (see [LAUNCH.md](LAUNCH.md)): 78 automated end-to-end browser test scripts covering stage, team, gear, cabling, layers, undo, save and reload, paperwork, printing and file export; axe-core accessibility audit clean in light and dark; phone, iPad and desktop layouts; Planning Center calls checked against the live Services API. Tested in Chromium; Safari and Firefox still need a pass.

Open `index.html` in a modern browser to run it.

## What it does

- **Stage designer**: on first use, draw your stage outline (click corners, drag to adjust, `+` on an edge adds a corner) or start from a rectangle, thrust, curved-front or angled-corner preset. Change it any time from **Room → Draw or edit stage shape**, optionally for every saved week. Edges facing the audience get the gold spike-tape line.
- **Stage & room**: set stage width/depth and house depth/width in metres. 50 cm grid, heavier line every 1 m (2×1 m deck panels). Rulers, upstage/downstage, stage left/right marked.
- **Planning Center import**: when opened in Claude (as a published artifact), pick a service type and plan, tick who's on stage, and the team is pulled from Services through your Planning Center connector. Positions map to stage roles automatically; people on two positions (e.g. Keys + Vocals) become "Keys, also sings".
- **Team → stage**: add this week's team by hand, or paste a schedule (`Name - Position` per line). Press **Build stage from team** and each person's gear is placed by position: drums + riser + shield, bass amp + DI, guitar amp + pedalboard, keys, tracks laptop, vocal mics, IEM packs, pulpit + headset, etc. Gear positions are remembered per stage role, so next week's drummer lands where you put this week's kit.
- **Gear library** (six categories: Instruments, Amps & FX, Mics & DI, Monitors & PA, Desks & tech, Stage & power; every item lives in exactly one; search looks across all): includes tablet stands (used instead of music stands when building from the team), acoustic preamp pedals, MIDI footswitch controllers, multi-effects floor units (stereo out to the stage box), MIDI keyboard controllers with a 2-in/2-out USB audio interface (keys players get both; Keys L/R patch from the interface), MultiTracks iPads and electronic drum kits; an Instruments tab with guitars on stands, trumpet, cajón and upright and grand pianos (mic'd instruments come with their inputs), plus mics, DIs, wedges, IEMs, mains, subs, flown line array hangs and line array subs (fed from the ceiling by default, like mains and subs; set how many boxes are in each hang from the inspector, and the power draw follows), stage boxes / digital snakes, FOH and monitor consoles, IEM racks, power distros, outlets, risers, cameras, lights. Every item has editable size, rotation, inputs, watts, owner.
- **Risers**: select a riser and drag a corner handle to resize it (10 cm steps, live size readout). Deck panels and legs are drawn at real size; set the riser height in the inspector.
- **Rack builder**: add a *Custom rack* (Gear → Snakes & desks, or Ctrl+K) and fill it from a library of rack gear: digital snakes, IEM transmitters, wireless receivers, patch bays, switches, amps, DSP, power and panels, plus custom devices. Pick the rack height, drag devices to reorder, add notes. A rack with a digital snake works as a stage box for patching; its IEM transmitters use its outputs; power is totalled. Racks get their own paperwork section.
- **Cable channels**: use the *Channel* tool (`T`) to draw floor trenches, cable trays or ramps. Auto-routed cables go through a channel when it keeps more of the run off the open floor; any cable can be set to run direct. Drop or drag gear onto a channel and it sits inside it (follows the channel if reshaped; hold Alt to place it on top instead; *Take out* in its details). Turn off *Inside channels* in Layers (or from a channel's details) to draw only the stretches of cable on the open stage (the channels themselves fade to a faint outline, and gear inside them hides too): dots mark where each cable drops into and out of a channel, channel labels show how many cables run inside, and a selected cable still shows its full path. Extend a channel with the + past either end (or start the Channel tool on its end); add a bend with the + on any edge.
- **Ceiling runs**: any cable can run along the ceiling (dashed on the plot); its length adds the climb up and down at each end using the ceiling height in Room. Speaker and sub feeds go overhead by default (toggle in Room). Draw an *Overhead truss* or *Ceiling tray* with the Channel tool for ceiling runs to follow. Hide or show ceiling runs from Layers.
- **Extension leads**: 2-way, 4-way and 6-way extension leads plug into a power point or distro, and gear plugs into them. Distros and power points are fed from the DB board, so nothing plugs into them from above (a lead between two of them is refused). Lead loads count against the feeding circuit, outlets and the lead's amp rating are checked, and leads plugged into other leads are flagged. Auto-cabling uses a lead when it's closer than mains and has a free outlet and headroom. The pull list shows each extension lead with its length.
- **Signal flow**: small arrows along every cable show which way the signal travels. Solid arrows are inputs (mics, DIs and instruments into a stage box or desk) and power (source to gear); open arrows are outputs (stage box out to wedges, speakers and IEMs); diamonds are digital-snake links that carry both ways. Selected cables show their stage-box ports (e.g. IN 12–13, OUT 3). Turn the arrows off, or animate the flow, from Layers (Advanced).
- **Cable runs**: XLR, Cat6 (digital snake), power, ¼″, Speakon, SDI/HDMI, USB (MIDI keyboards and controllers to the USB interface, iPad or laptop; auto-cabled). Run by hand: Run cable → click the start gear → each click on the floor is a pivot, with the leg into it locked straight (horizontal or vertical) → click the destination gear and the last leg turns into it. A live preview shows pivots, length and the target; Clicking a channel while running a cable enters it at that point; the cable follows the channel and comes out where you click it again (or at the point nearest the destination), and keeps following the channel if you reshape it. Backspace undoes the last pivot, Esc cancels. Dragging a pivot later snaps it into line with its neighbours. Or use auto-cabling or auto-cable: inputs → nearest stage box with free ports, power → least-loaded circuit on nearest distro, stage boxes → FOH over Cat6. Lengths follow the floor at right angles plus slack % and drop at each end, then round up to metric stock lengths.
- **Power distro**: circuits × amps × volts per distro (default 16 A @ 230 V); per-circuit load meters; warnings at 80% and over breaker.
- **Paperwork**: one sheet at a time, picked from tabs: **Overview** (a clean drawing of the stage plot, plus anything to check before load-in), Inputs (with stage box port patching), Outputs, Monitor mixes, Cable pull list, Power, Team and Racks. *Everything* shows them all on one page, and printing always includes every sheet. Each table copies straight into a spreadsheet.
- **People only** (Team tab, above the Build button): untick *Add each person’s instruments & gear* and building from the team places just the people; you add instruments, mics and monitors yourself. Unticking removes the gear it had added (undoable), new team members arrive without gear, and the choice is remembered in this browser. Tick it again to bring the gear back.
- **Clear this week** (week menu, or Ctrl+K): *Keep the stage* removes the team, gear, cables, channels and notes but keeps the stage shape, size and colour and the room settings; *Clear everything* also resets the stage to a blank 10 × 6 m rectangle. Both ask for a second click and can be undone (Undo in the notice or Ctrl+Z). The date and name stay.
- **On the day**: Home's *Next up* card has *Print or PDF* and a *Load-in* checklist (with progress). The checklist (also a Paperwork tab) lists every power lead, the snake, every input, every instrument lead and every monitor or output, each with where it goes and how long the cable is; tick them off as they're done (big tap targets for phones). Ticks save with the week straight away and survive a reload; *Clear ticks* starts over.
- **What changed**: the paperwork overview opens with *What's different from* last week: who joined or is off, gear added or removed, the input count and anything moved more than 50 cm. Crews can read it before load-in.
- **To borrow**: with owned counts set in Home → Gear, the overview lists what a week needs beyond what you own, and the Home card shows *Borrow N*.
- **Notes on people and gear**: any item (people included) takes a short note in its details: a small badge marks it on the plot (hover to read it) and the paperwork lists every note.
- **Gear finds its owner**: personal gear (instruments, mics, DIs, pedals, monitors) dropped within 1.5 m of a person is assigned to them, with *Not theirs* in the notice to undo it. Shared gear (stage boxes, distros, speakers, risers…) never is.
- **Setups by service type**: link a setup to a Planning Center service type (Home → Setups). Importing a plan of that type into an empty week lays the stage out from that setup first.
- **Library editing**: the pen on a library row renames a week or moves it to another date in place (Undo in the notice).
- **Keys**: `G` goes Home, `P` toggles Paperwork; both are in Ctrl K too, along with the load-in checklist.
- **Pro input list**: every channel shows the mic or DI, its stand (tall boom, short boom, clip, inside drum, lectern, handheld), whether it needs 48V phantom power, a group (Drums, Bass, Guitars, Acoustic, Keys, Playback, Vocals, Speech, Horns) and a scribble-strip colour. *CSV* downloads the full list; *Desk names* downloads channel, short name (person's name dropped, 12 characters), colour and phantom, ready to type into or import to a desk.
- **Fixed channel numbers** (on by default): every input keeps its desk channel from week to week, so saved desk scenes stay right. Inputs are known by stage role and name (Drums · Kick In, Vocalist · Vox 2), not by person, so a new drummer lands on the same channels; someone away leaves their channels spare, and the input list shows each spare one and whose it usually is. *Edit channels* on the input list lets you type a channel number (one already taken swaps the two, Undo in the notice), correct the mic or DI, pick the stand and tick 48V; those changes follow the input to every week. *Renumber in band order* starts the numbering again; untick *Keep channel numbers fixed* to number by band order each week. Channel changes are undoable (Ctrl Z, or Undo in the notice) and appear in Version history; restoring a version brings its channel numbers back too. *Desk names* lists spare channels as *Spare* so every scribble strip lines up. Saved with your weeks and in full backups.
- **My spot** (Paperwork tab): a card for each person with the plot (their gear lit, everything else faded, a YOU ring), where they stand in words (stage left, mid-stage, 3.7 m from the front edge, on the drum riser), who's on their left and right, their channels with mic and stand, their monitor mix, where each of their things plugs in (distro and circuit), their gear by its full name (*Electric guitar*, *In-ear monitor pack*) and their notes, with *Arrive by*, *Soundcheck* and *Service* times on top. Set the times in the week menu, or they come from Planning Center with the plan (earliest time of the day, first rehearsal, first service). The YOU tag sits on a short stalk clear of the gear. *Copy text* gives a plain message to send them; *Print every card* prints one card per page. Works at phone width.

A blank week shows a small drawing with *Add the team*, *Add starter gear*, *Browse gear* and any saved setups to start from.

Lights on stands are fixtures in a yoke with a warm beam, and a light placed out in the house comes aimed at the stage. Home, Plot and Paperwork fade in when you switch (off with reduced motion). A vocal mic more than 30 cm from its singer shows a boom line to them. Dark mode: the house is a shade lighter and the deck has an edge, so the stage stands off it; the paperwork header has a subtle edge and gradient.

## Stage-plot symbols

Every gear item is drawn as a real top-down symbol (drum kit with shells and cymbals, mic stands with booms, consoles with fader strips, stage boxes with XLR ports, distros with sockets, etc.). They live in `index.html` and are also exported as standalone files in [`symbols/`](symbols/README.md). After editing a symbol, run `node tools/export-symbols.mjs` to refresh the files.

## Paperwork

- **Floor marks** (Paperwork tab): where to put tape before load-in. People, risers and floor-standing gear, measured from the stage-right edge and back from the front edge, front of stage first. Risers are marked by their front stage-right corner, with their size.
- **Stays in storage** (bottom of the Load-in checklist): owned gear (Home → Gear) this week doesn't use, so nobody carries it out for nothing.
- **Save plot as picture** (paperwork view bar, ⋯ menu or Ctrl+K): a sharp PNG of the stage plot with the service name, date, venue and times across the top, ready for a group chat.
- The stage plot on the paperwork always draws every layer and every cable at full strength, without flow arrows or pulses, so it prints clean.
- The input list groups rows by person: the name shows once and a firmer rule marks each new person.
- Power tables share fixed columns, so circuits, loads and meters line up from one distro to the next.
- **Print or PDF** (top right of the paperwork, or Ctrl+P from anywhere): every sheet prints on its own page with no app chrome, tables keep their headings across pages, and colours print true. Choose *Save as PDF* in the print dialog for a file.
- Outputs list box by box, port by port.
- **Download sheets** saves every sheet as one self-contained web page (stage plot included) that opens, shares and prints anywhere without the app. *Download .json* in Backup & restore saves all weeks. Inside Claude both ask you to confirm the save; elsewhere they're ordinary downloads.
- **About Stageplot** (⋯ menu): version, where your data lives (this browser only), and what the optional Planning Center link reads (service types, plans and who's scheduled; read-only).

## Reliability and accessibility

- If saved data ever can't be read, it is never overwritten: a copy is kept in the browser and a **Recover** button opens it in Backup & restore.
- An unexpected error shows one calm notice instead of failing silently; work already saved stays saved.
- Checked with axe-core in light and dark themes: no violations (contrast at least 4.5:1, labelled panels and tabs, keyboard-reachable canvas, a page heading for screen readers).

## Saving

Weeks auto-save in the browser (localStorage). Use **Save / load** to copy or download JSON as a backup or to move to another computer.

## Using it

- **Ready-made teams**: on an empty Team tab (or Home → Setups) pick *Acoustic trio*, *Full band*, *Kids worship* or *Speaker only*. The positions are placed on stage straight away; type the names in afterwards.
- **Name suggestions**: typing a name in the Team tab suggests people from earlier weeks. Picking someone whose position is still empty fills in the position they had last time (and whether they sing).
- **Show mode** (`F`, or ⋯ → Present on a screen): the plot full screen with bigger, bolder labels and nothing popping up over it. Tap a person and a large card shows where they stand, their channels and their monitor; tapping never moves anything. Esc closes the card, then show mode.
- **Hover card**: the size only shows for risers and speakers, where it matters.
- **Colour**: cool modern neutrals with an indigo-violet accent for buttons, selection and focus; amber spike tape marks only the stage's front edge. Cables use a clean, distinct set (XLR blue, Cat6 purple, power orange, jack green, Speakon teal, video pink, USB slate), with brighter versions in dark mode. Text and chips keep at least 4.5:1 contrast in both themes.
- **Workspace**: the stage fills the middle. Left: Team, Gear library and Room. Right: details for whatever is selected, or the week guide and stage settings when nothing is. Bottom dock: **Add** (`A`, opens the library with search ready), Move, Run cable, Measure, **Layers**, zoom and fit. The Select tool joins the dock on touch screens and in Advanced (with a mouse, Shift-drag does the same; `S` always works).
- **Setup guide**: with nothing selected the right panel walks through six steps: Stage → Team → Gear → Audio → Power → Review & share. Each tick comes from the plan itself (stage sized or drawn, people added, gear placed, nothing unpatched, nothing unpowered, no checks left), never from which screens you opened. Work in any order; click a step to jump to where that work happens (Audio and Power show just those cables). The current step carries its action buttons. Hide the guide with its ×; bring it back from the **⋯** menu. While the guide is showing, checks live in its last step and the status pill; with it hidden, the panel lists the first three.
- **Stage settings with nothing selected** (the one place to type them; the Room tab keeps the shape drawing): width and depth (a custom shape stretches to fit), snap size for moving gear (5 cm, 25 cm, 50 cm grid or off; hold Alt to place freely), *Edit shape*, **stage colour** (Charcoal, Black, Slate, Navy, Walnut, Oak, Light, or any colour from the picker; light decks switch the grid, stage words, riser outlines, drum-shield plexi and faded cables to dark ink so they stay visible), and the week's inputs, cable and power at a glance. The colour is saved with the week, carried by setups and *Copy this week*, shown on the Room tab's thumbnail and printed on the paperwork plot.
- **Layers** (dock): show or hide People, Gear, Labels, Rulers & stage words and the Floor grid, every cable type on the plot, ceiling runs and cables inside channels, with *All / Audio / Power* focus buttons. Hiding only changes the view: nothing in the plan is touched, the paperwork always draws everything, and anything you select stays visible. When something is hidden the dock shows how many and a bar over the stage names them, with **Show all** one click away. The cable key (input, output, digital, overhead) sits at the bottom of the menu.
- **People on the plot** take their role's colour, matching the team list: voices rose, band sky blue, pastor/speaker mint, tech slate, each with a white ring so they stand off instruments and cables. The stage casts a soft shadow, the 50 cm grid lines are fainter than the metre lines, and overhead runs stay faint until you select something.
- **Calmer chrome**: once the stage is built, *Rebuild stage from team* is a plain button so the one gold button on screen is the next step; panel toggles in the header use grey, not gold; speaker coverage fans are fainter.
- **Electric guitar types**: select an electric guitar and pick its type in the details panel: black offset double-cut, black single-cut, white single-cut or white hollow body. Guitars, the bass and the acoustic are cut-out photos (embedded as small WebP files) sized to each instrument's real length, with a soft shadow on the deck; the picker shows the same photos, undo works, and the next guitar you add uses the type you picked last. Weeks saved with older guitar types move to the closest new one. No brand names are used for any gear.
- **Mic types**: vocal, wireless and instrument mics are vector drawings in the same style (mesh grilles drawn as chords, chrome bands, glints) and each has a type picker in its details: vocal (dynamic, silver, vintage, studio, lectern gooseneck), wireless (handheld, red handheld, lapel), instrument (side-address on short stand, condenser on tripod, desk/boundary). Mics have no backing disc: a faint light rim and a soft shadow keep even a black mic readable on a dark stage, and they draw over their singer, as if held. All instrument and mic drawings share one set of gradients and blur filters, embedded once in the page, in downloaded paperwork and in each exported symbol. New mics use the type picked last.
- **Bass effects pedal (2-channel)** in Gear → Band: a small pedal with a knob row and footswitch per channel; *Run cable* picks Jack to and from basses, amps, DIs and other pedals.
- **DI boxes** are drawn like the real thing from above: steel bookend caps, a blue top with ground-lift and pad toggles, the 1/4" jack input at the left end and the XLR output (pins and latch) at the right; the stereo DI has two of each. Pedals show their side jacks.
- **Venues** (Home → Venues): plan for more than one room (*Main auditorium*, *Next Gen hall*, *Summer camp*). Each venue keeps its own stage (size, shape, colour, surface, house, ceiling) and the setup its new weeks start from. Every week belongs to one venue: *New week* on a venue starts there, the week menu moves a week to another venue (it takes that venue's stage, keeping its gear), and the week arrows step through that venue's weeks. In the Room panel a line shows the venue and, when this week's stage has been changed, offers *Save to venue* or *Reset*. The Library filters by venue and tags each week; the paperwork, title block and rider name the venue. Venues travel with backups; a library from before venues becomes one venue (*Main stage*) holding every week. A venue can be deleted once it has no weeks.
- **Your logo**: upload it from Home (the *Add your church logo* button, or Stage defaults → Church logo). It sits beside the app name in the header and at the top of Home, as well as on the paperwork, rider and My spot cards.
- **Feet and inches** (Home → Preferences → Units): every size, distance and cable length on the plot, the paperwork and the rider reads in feet and inches (*32′ 10″ × 19′ 8″*, *28′ out*), fields take feet, the rulers number every 5 ft, the grid is 1 ft squares with 5 ft lines, snap sizes become 1 in / 6 in / 1 ft, and the cable pull list uses US stock lengths (25, 50, 100 ft…). Plans are stored the same either way, so switching back and forth changes nothing.
- **Focus a person**: picking a performer on their own dims everything that isn't theirs (gear, cables, names and folded badges), so their spot reads at a glance.
- **Role marks on the plot**: each performer's disc carries the same small position icon as the team list, kept upright when they turn.
- **Empty sheets explain themselves**: an empty input list, outputs, monitor mixes, cable pull list or team sheet says why and offers the one button that fills it (hidden when printed).
- **Pinch to zoom on touch screens**: two fingers zoom the plot on iPads and phones instead of zooming the whole page; one finger still scrolls and drags gear.
- **Lift while dragging**: gear in hand rises a touch off the deck with a deeper shadow, and settles with a small bounce when you let go (no motion with reduced motion).
- **When printing is blocked**: some places that embed Stageplot (the Claude preview among them) don't allow the print dialog. If it doesn't open, a note offers the sheets as a file instead (just the rider, or just the My spot cards, when that's what you were printing); open it and use its *Print or save as PDF* button, which doesn't appear on paper.
- **Sheet tabs**: one quiet row of words (Overview, Rider, My spot · Inputs, Outputs, Monitor mixes, Cables, Power, Racks · Team, Load-in · Everything), the open sheet underlined in the accent colour; Overview shows a small amber dot when there are things to check. *Print or PDF* sits at the end of the row; under it, a quiet line holds **View: Pages | Scroll** (wide screens) and **Download all sheets**. On narrow screens the row scrolls with a soft fade at the edge.
- **Paperwork as pages**: on a wide screen the sheets show as white A4 pages on a grey desk, numbered, so you see what will print, in dark mode too. **View: Scroll** under the tabs turns it off (remembered in this browser).
- **Library trends**: each week in the Library has a tiny line for its inputs (solid) and team size (dashed) over up to six weeks ending with that week; hover it for the numbers.
- **Title block**: under the overview plot on the paperwork, a drawing-style strip names the venue, service, date, stage size, revision, who drew it and when it was printed. Set *Drawn by* (a name and number) in Home → Stage defaults.
- **Planning Center while loading**: soft grey placeholder rows in the shape of what's coming (roster rows, or a picker) pulse beside the status line instead of a bare spinner.
- **Picking cables in a bunch**: a click picks the cable nearest the pointer (not just the one drawn on top), and pointing at a run lights the nearest one. Where several run side by side, click again on the same spot to step to the next; a note says which one you have (*Cable 2 of 4 here: Casey Kit → Stage box*). Right-click works the same way.
- **Instruments in colour**: in the Calm look, guitars, drums, keys and other instruments keep their full colour; only the rest of the gear is toned down a little until you point at it.
- **Getting started card**: until a week other than the example has a team, Home opens with a calm card (the welcome screen's stage drawing and three steps that tick off: draw your stage, add the team, build and print) and one button for the next step; *Hide this* puts it away.
- **Team list in sections**: Band, Voices, Tech, then *No position yet* (where new rows land), each with a count.
- **Gear cards** show each item's size, and an *own 2* badge when you've set how many you own (amber when this week uses more).
- **Load-in ring**: the Load-in button on Home shows a small ring filling as the checklist is ticked (green when done).
- **Status pulse**: when the number of checks changes, the status chip gives one soft pulse (none with reduced motion).
- **Printed page footer**: every printed page carries the week name, date and church (and venue) bottom-left, and *Page 2 of 9* bottom-right.
- **The next 8 service days** (Home → Library): a strip of your usual service day for the next eight weeks, each with a dot: filled when planned, a ring when started but empty, a dash when free. Click a planned day to open it, a free one to start a week there; with a venue picked in the Library it shows that venue only.
- **Next up card**: a calm two-part card on Home: the week's stage drawn large on the left; on the right the date (and venue), name, status, team, figures and the buttons to open the plot, paperwork, print or load-in. On wide screens Home spreads out to use the room; settings pages keep a readable width.
- **Next up figures**: Home's Next up card shows people, items, inputs, metres of cable and kW, counting up briefly when Home opens (once, not on every redraw; off with reduced motion).
- **Team list**: each avatar carries a small outline mark for the person's position (mic for voices, guitar, keys, drums, laptop for tracks, speech bubble for the pastor, desk, headphones, bulb or clipboard for tech), rows are split by fine lines instead of boxes, and the *Also sings* mic stays faint until it's on or you point at the row.
- **Names that make room**: gear and people names sit on a soft rounded tag below the item; when that spot is taken they try above, then beside it (right, then left), so a crowded name moves aside instead of disappearing or landing on other gear. Small gear names hide when zoomed out.
- **Team on the plot**: each person is a soft disc in their role's colour with light from above, a fine inner ring, a shadow on the deck and a small notch facing where they face; initials take a deeper shade of the role colour. Team list avatars match.
- **Gear details**: name, then a compact Rotate / Lock / Duplicate / Delete bar, then connections. *Belongs to* sits up front for people and for gear someone owns; shared gear (stage boxes, distros, speakers) keeps it under More settings. People don't get a Connections list unless a cable actually runs to them.
- **Cable details**: the panel names the cable family (Signal, Power, Video) and type, then **From** and **To** in the direction the signal or power travels; click either to jump to that gear. Length and stock to pull sit below, then **Route**: *On stage* or *Overhead*, and *Use channels* or *Direct* when the room has channels, with a line saying which channel it runs through. Running a power lead to gear that draws no power gives a heads-up (nothing is blocked).
- **Simple and Advanced views**: Simple (the default) keeps everyday controls up front: label, owner, rotate/lock/duplicate/delete, connections, riser size, line-array boxes, power load meters, cable type and floor/ceiling, stage size and crew notes. Exact positions, sizes, power draw, input names, ports, circuit ratings, cable slack and routing fold away under **More settings** (folds you open stay open as you click around). The Channel tool and the flow-arrow and animation switches in Layers appear in Advanced. Switch from the **⋯** menu or Ctrl+K. The choice is remembered.
- **Run a cable from here**: unconnected gear offers a button to start a cable from it straight away.
- **Phones**: a two-row header (logo, search, Home / Plot / Paperwork, undo, redo, ⋯; then the week); the plot fits the stage edge to edge; selecting anything puts its details straight under the plot; notices appear at the top so they never cover the team list.
- **Snake to FOH**: the auto Cat6 run from an on-stage box to an off-stage desk leaves by the nearer side of the stage and runs along the front, never across the deck (unless you've drawn a cable channel, which it rides instead).
- **Tablets**: on iPad-sized screens the plot fills the screen when the details panel is tucked away, and the tool dock drops its labels when the canvas is narrow.
- **Tidy header**: view level (Simple / Advanced), theme, the setup guide, keyboard shortcuts and backup live in one **⋯** menu at the top right. Once the week has a team, Planning Center shrinks to a single *Update the team* line; the right panel shows *All set* instead of the step list when everything's done and keeps the first three checks (*See all* opens the full list).
- **Instrument leads** (jack cables) lie like real leads: one calm, relaxed curve from plug to plug (a gentle C or S, its belly about a fifth of the run), or one flowing line through any bends you place. Leads plug in where real ones do and leave the socket the way it faces: at the bottom centre of a guitar's body, into a pedal's IN on its right-hand side and out of its OUT on the left (pedalboard, bass pedal, multi-FX, acoustic preamp; chained pedals go OUT to IN), and into a DI's jack input at its left end. Leads in a cable channel follow the channel with their corners eased. They're a little heavier than other cables, with a crisp dark edge and a light core; a lead you run by hand draws itself on. Each lead's curve comes from its id, so no two look stamped out. Only the drawing changes: lengths, patching and saved routes are the same.
- **Cable drawing**: line weights are set in screen pixels (about 2 px, 1.3 px while quiet, 3 px selected) and capped so they never outgrow their lanes, so runs stay fine and crisp at any zoom; flow arrows and ceiling dashes scale with them. Runs turn corners with smooth rounded bends (short jogs stay true), and every floor run on the stage sits in a thin deck-coloured sheath, so where cables cross the one on top reads cleanly over the other. Runs you're looking at draw last; the selected run gets a soft glow in its colour and highlighted runs a fine light core, so they read as cable rather than line. The flow animation follows the rounded path.
- **Tidy cabling on the plot**: cables that share a run are drawn side by side in lanes (power, then XLR, jack, USB, Speakon, Cat6, video) instead of on top of each other; gear sits above the cables so plugs disappear under it; and with nothing selected, cables go quiet (thin, faded, no arrows) so the stage reads first. Select gear or a cable to bring its runs, arrows, lengths and port badges forward. Item names never print over other gear: a name that would land on something moves above its item, and if both spots are taken it's left off (people always keep theirs). Names, lengths and port badges that would overlap each other are thinned out, keeping the selection, then people, then big gear. Selected gear is drawn in front, so a mic never hides under its singer. People, mics, headsets, lights and cameras are selected with a ring that hugs them rather than a square, and a selected name lifts clear of the ring and rotate knob. Stage words (upstage, downstage, stage left/right) only show where no gear or riser covers them, and riser captions only where no gear does; while cables are quiet, ceiling runs tuck under the gear instead of crossing speaker names and symbols; a selected riser always shows its size and height. In Simple, the status pill sticks to *Ready* / checks and the input count. Turn *Quiet until selected* off, or show *Audio only* / *Power only*, from Layers.
- **Checks you can act on**: click the status pill (e.g. *3 checks*) for a list of what needs attention. Each check has **Show** (jumps to and selects the gear) and, where it can be fixed for you, a fix button: *Patch & power it* (re-runs cabling), *Bring it back* (gear outside the drawing), *Pick a position* (team member with no position). *Patch & power everything* fixes all cabling checks at once. The same buttons appear in the right panel and the paperwork.
- **New gear arrives patched**: adding, duplicating or pasting gear re-runs auto-cabling, so a new mic lands patched to a stage box and new powered gear gets a power run, with nothing to press. Cables you ran or reshaped by hand stay as they are. Turn it off in Room → More room settings → *Patch and power new gear automatically*.
- **Auto cable type**: *Run cable* starts on **Auto**, which picks the cable from the two ends: power from a distro, power point or extension lead; Cat6 between stage boxes and desks; jack from guitars and between pedals, amps and DIs; USB from MIDI gear to an interface, iPad or laptop; video for cameras; XLR otherwise. The preview names the type before you click. Pick a type chip to force one.
- **Click to place**: click gear in the library (or a recent chip) and it follows the pointer as a ghost; click the stage to drop it exactly there (onto a channel puts it inside). Shift-click to place several, Esc or *Cancel* to put it back. The empty-stage card steps aside while you place. Dragging still works. Adding gear no longer pops a message; Undo in the header covers it.
- **Gear library**: the six categories sit in a two-across grid with a small icon each, so all of them are always in view (no sideways scrolling); the last one you used is remembered. *Recent* is one row of your last five items as icon buttons. Tiles reflow to fit the panel (two across in the side panel, three on wider screens) with the icon, the full name (never cut off), the name (bracket notes dropped unless they tell two items apart, e.g. wired / wireless mic), and what it plugs into or draws in small type; hover for the full name. Each list is headed with how to use it: tap, then tap the stage, or drag. While searching, the categories give way to a count of matches. A scripted audit checks every category at desktop, laptop and phone widths for hidden categories, cut-off names, sideways scrolling and targets under 32 px. Icons are framed on the drawing itself rather than the footprint, so long or small gear fills its tile like everything else, and speaker coverage fans are left out of icons. Drawn for recognition: the guitars, bass and acoustic are cut-out photos at real length; guitar icons lean across their tile framed on the body, while the plot keeps them straight and to scale; the acoustic kit has wine-lacquer shells with chrome hoops and lugs, coated heads, a kick on its side with spurs and pedal, bronze cymbals with lathe rings and a bright bell, and a leather throne on a rug; the electronic kit has a black tube rack, mesh pads, rubber cymbals, a kick tower and its module; the drum shield is five plexi panels on an arc wrapping the kit, the music stand carries an open part, the confidence monitor shows lyrics, the trumpet shows its tubing loop, valves and bell, the grand piano has its lid open on a gold plate and strings, the MIDI controller is a dark body with wheels, pads and knobs (so it isn't mistaken for the keyboard rig), risers read as lit deck panels and the drum riser icon carries a kit.
- **Recently used gear**: the Gear tab keeps your last eight added items in a row at the top; click or drag them like any gear.
- **Setups**: save a stage layout under a name (e.g. *Full band*, *Acoustic*, *Youth night*) from the week menu, then lay out any week the same way with one click. Team gear keeps its spot by position, so this week's drummer lands where the kit was; the stage shape, channels, risers, infrastructure and hand-run cables come across, and cabling re-runs. Undo puts the week back. Setups are included in *Backup all weeks*.
- **Select several**: Shift-click gear (or Ctrl/Cmd-click) to add or remove it, Shift-drag the floor to box-select, Ctrl+A for all gear (risers left out). Drag any selected item to move the lot; nudge, rotate, duplicate, delete or reassign them together, and line them up (left, centre, right, upstage, downstage) or space them evenly from the panel. A plain click on one narrows the selection to it.
- **Select tool** (`S`): for touch and trackpads, no Shift needed. Tap gear to pick or drop it, drag a box over the floor, drag any picked item to move them all; tap empty floor to clear.
- **Measure** (`M`): drag (or click, then click) between two points for the distance in metres, with across / up-and-down parts for diagonals. Gear snaps to its centre; Shift keeps the line level. It stays until Esc or another tool.
- **Rotate by hand**: drag the knob above selected gear (15° steps, Alt for any angle). While dragging gear, its distance from stage right and upstage shows live.
- **What changed since last week**: from the *This week* panel (or Ctrl+K) highlight gear that's new (green), moved (amber, with an arrow from where it was) or removed (red outline where it was) compared with the previous week.
- **Lock in place**: lock gear that never moves (FOH, distros, risers, stage boxes) from its details, the right-click menu or `L`. Locked gear can't be dragged, nudged, rotated or lined up by accident, shows a padlock when selected, and stays locked when you rebuild from the team.
- **Copy and paste**: Ctrl+C / Ctrl+X / Ctrl+V copies gear with the cables between it; pastes land under the pointer, and work across weeks (copy a band setup from one Sunday into another).
- **Search or jump to (Ctrl+K)**: run any action, add any gear by name, or find a person or item on stage.
- **Right-click** gear or a cable for quick actions (edit, run a cable from here, rotate, duplicate, assign to someone, hide that cable type, delete). **Double-click** gear to edit its details. Hover for a quick info card.
- Dragging gear snaps to line up with other gear and the stage centreline (pink guides). Hold Alt to move freely.
- A "Saved" tick in the header confirms every change is stored.
- **Cables in Layers**: one switch shows or hides every cable (also `H`), *All / Audio / Power* quick views, then only the cable types this plot actually uses, each with its count and an eye to hide it, plus ceiling runs and cables inside channels. *Display* holds *Quiet until selected*; Advanced adds flow arrows and flow animation. Animated flow pulses move in step on every cable, and keep moving while cables are quiet. Your choices stay in your browser.
- Hide the left or right panel with the header buttons, `[` and `]`, or press `\` for focus mode (both hidden). The left panel shrinks to an icon strip; with the right panel hidden, a small card over the plot keeps rotate / duplicate / delete for whatever you select.
- Drag the floor to pan; pinch or Ctrl+scroll to zoom. **Stage** / **Room** buttons refit the view.
- Drag gear from the Gear tab onto the stage, or click it to drop centre stage.
- Every change can be undone; toasts offer one-click undo. Theme follows your system or can be set with the half-circle button.
